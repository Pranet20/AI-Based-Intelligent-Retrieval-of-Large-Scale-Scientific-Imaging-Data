# Abstract (V2 Revised Draft — Post-Audit Remediation)

Scientific image repositories frequently suffer from severe metadata fragmentation, acquisition-induced visual distortions, and uncurated specimen anomalies, impeding reproducible cross-laboratory retrieval. In this work, we present an end-to-end platform for automated metadata extraction, self-supervised visual representation, and integrity-aware curation of electron microscopy archives. 

Evaluating over 5,365 scanning electron microscopy (SEM) micrographs, we establish that self-supervised Vision Transformers (DINOv2 ViT-S/14) achieved higher top-1 retrieval performance than the evaluated ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 baseline on the held-out Zeiss Gemini benchmark (R@1 = 0.9481 vs. 0.9245; MRR = 0.9658 vs. 0.9542), while ResNet-50 maintained higher broader recall (R@5 = 1.0000). Supervised contrastive adaptation yielded the highest top-5 precision density (P@5 = 0.9053). Linear and non-linear multimodal fusion benchmarks demonstrate that conditioning the visual representation on the evaluated acquisition metadata configuration reduced retrieval performance (R@1 dropped to 0.5896–0.6274), indicating that the tested metadata acted as a confounding factor on this benchmark. 

To support repository integrity, the platform deploys automated blur and artifact triage; under controlled synthetic perturbations, retrieval showed 90–96% relative retention under scale-bar incursions and JPEG compression, while severe defocus blur reduced retention to 48%. Double-blind expert review across 100 triage events showed substantial inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's Kappa $\kappa = 0.842$) for expert-identified novelty and quality cases, with an average review latency of 42.5 seconds. In an engineering stress test evaluating vector-index behavior, FAISS HNSW maintained sub-millisecond query latency (0.317 ms; 15.64x speedup over flat search) up to 100,000 vectors. The platform passed 218/218 unit/integration tests natively on the host platform, offering a hardened foundation for scientific data stewardship.
