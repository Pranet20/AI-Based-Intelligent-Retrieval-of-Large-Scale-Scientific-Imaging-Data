# ABSTRACT

Scientific imaging across electron microscopy produces voluminous, high-dimensional micrograph repositories characterized by heterogeneous acquisition settings, noisy or fragmented instrument metadata, and uncurated image redundancies. Conventional image data management workflows rely on manual folder hierarchies and manual inspections that fail to scale, lack acquisition invariance, and provide inadequate mechanisms for data integrity assessment or anomaly triage. 

In this work, we present an integrated, reproducibility-engineered scientific image data management platform designed for scanning electron microscopy (SEM). The proposed framework unifies end-to-end ingestion, cryptographic provenance tracking, automated data integrity assessment, perceptual duplicate detection, acquisition-robust representation learning, sub-millisecond approximate nearest neighbor retrieval, decoupled metadata filtering, embedding-space novelty screening, and active human-in-the-loop curation.

Using a frozen 22M-parameter self-supervised Vision Transformer (DINOv2 ViT-S/14), the platform extracts 384-dimensional representations that achieve a top-1 retrieval accuracy ($R@1$) of 0.9481 and Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) of 0.9658 on an in-domain ferrous metallurgy corpus (High-Chromium Cast Iron, $N=774$), outperforming supervised ImageNet baselines ($R@1=0.9245$). A multi-head Supervised Contrastive (SupCon) adaptation head trained across accelerating voltage and detector variations significantly mitigates acquisition-induced retrieval bias, achieving a 68.15% bias gap reduction ($p = 1.42 \times 10^{-12}$, paired $t$-test) and Precision@5 ($P@5$) of 0.9053. High-dimensional vector indexing via Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW) graphs preserves high recall while maintaining query latencies between 0.096 ms and 0.317 ms across vector scale. Automated data integrity assessment evaluates image-derived focus/quality indicators on the declared controlled quality-screening benchmark via Tenengrad gradient energy (AUROC 0.8803, AUPRC 0.9618); the HCCI corpus contained 0 bitwise-exact duplicate pairs (natural redundancy graph contained 769 clusters comprising 764 singleton clusters and 5 two-image near-duplicate/review clusters), with $F_1 = 0.9810$ on the controlled perturbation duplicate benchmark.

Evaluating cross-domain generalization without retraining on a previously evaluated defect SEM corpus (Carinthia, $N=4,591$, 6 classes) demonstrates zero-shot nearest-neighbor class consistency of Micro $R@1 = 0.9952$ and Macro $R@1 = 0.9090$, descriptively outperforming zero-shot CLIP ViT-B/32 (Micro $R@1 = 0.7840$) and supervised ResNet-50 (Micro $R@1 = 0.6420$). End-to-end held-out batch ingestion operates at 14.80 images/second with complete SHA-256 provenance logging, and double-blinded human curation triage achieves an actionable curation yield of 91.67% (110/120 cases confirmed, Cohen's $\kappa = 0.8420$).

We explicitly declare six system boundaries: remote cloud deployment and live container engine execution were not executed due to host infrastructure boundaries; physical Energy-Dispersive X-Ray Spectroscopy (EDS) validation was not executed (synthetic spectra serve data pipeline testing only); upstream restricted research datasets are distributed via metadata manifests; comparative CLIP/ResNet baselines are descriptive point estimates whose local reproduction is not verified; and representation robustness breaks down under severe synthetic defocus blur ($\sigma \ge 2.0$), requiring pre-retrieval quality filtering. The platform, schemas, and 145 frozen research artifacts are released under an open reproducible framework.

**Keywords**: Scientific Image Management, Scanning Electron Microscopy, Self-Supervised Vision Transformer, Contrastive Learning, Cross-Acquisition Retrieval, Data Integrity Assessment, Human-in-the-Loop Curation.
